Co-Cd activity investigation — 2026-10-03

The Co-Cd deficit is selective. The leading explanation is a measured-background input/convention difference, rather than a general Co-60 isotope error or arithmetic error. This investigation changed no production code or inputs.

Coverage
All 12 recovered monitor counts were inspected: 10 original ASC reductions and two original native ANS diagnostics. Native counts were hash-bound; live-time offsets were cross-checked against 30 ASC files. Cu-Cd uses its report's 25 cm calibration parameters without importing QG peak counts or activities. Fe-Cd retains its separate near-contact calibration and is excluded from the 25 cm population. No ASC files were invented.

There are 19 paired measurement/isotope results. Excluding three Sc-48 report-convention cases and one near-contact case leaves 15 diagnostic comparisons; seven exceed a 20% difference screen across six count files. The three Ti counts are repeated measurements, not independent specimens.

Cobalt calculation
At 1173/1332 keV, activity uses 6,989.5/6,974.5 physical net counts, while the exported raw comparison uses 10,522/11,020. QG reports 11,202/11,439. Thus the earlier observation that counts agree referred to a different count field from the one feeding activity.

For the first line:
  6989.5 / (129600 * 0.0003554891539202694 * 0.9985) = 151.938097 Bq.
The second line gives 159.138099 Bq. Their inverse-variance average is 156.231075 Bq; QG's summary is 234.95 Bq. The conversion arithmetic closes exactly.

The implementation deliberately preserves raw-spectrum comparison counts separately from physical counts after measured-background subtraction and local residual-continuum estimation. Two workflow calls compute adjusted data for export and analysis separately from the original spectrum; this is not sequential double subtraction. Local continuum subtraction is a separate operation, not proof that the measured background was subtracted twice.

For bare Co, historical North gives 2,093.296 Bq, no measured background gives 2,188.852 Bq, and QG gives 2,035 Bq. The measured-background operation changes both cobalt estimates by about 90–96 Bq, a much larger fraction of the weaker Cd-covered signal. South substitution alone does not resolve the discrepancy.

The North file is labeled North and dated March 2026; the native South file is dated October 3, 2025, after the August measurements. Both contain Co-60 peaks. QG reports do not identify a separate background file. Neither later background nor the no-background control is accepted here as the correct physical background. The controls support a convention/applicability difference, not a claim that QG omitted every ambient correction.

Math and timing checks
All 41 checked line calculations close exactly with recorded inputs; 21 isotope combinations close within 2.22e-16 relative error. Live times match QG/native inputs. For Co-Cd, live versus real normalization changes by 0.071%, and count-average to count-start correction by 0.027%. Clock discrepancies and EOI/activation/mass calculations cannot explain the 33.5% measurement-time deficit. Co-60 yields agree. Legacy efficiencies are about 6–11% below QG-effective values, which pushes activity upward at fixed counts, opposite to this deficit. No global efficiency multiplier is justified.

Other families
Cu-64 agrees within about 1–3% in both bare and Cd counts. Sc-46 pooled results remain within 20% across five counts, although individual line effects and weighted cancellation matter.

Indium, Co-58 in Ni, and Sc-47 in Ti show positive differences. Their dominant physical peak counts are mostly near QG, while the legacy efficiency is lower. Recovered-curve/legacy efficiency ratios are about 1.57 at 159 keV, 1.56 at 190 keV, 1.44 at 336 keV, 1.32 at 558 keV and 1.22 at 810 keV, compared with 1.09/1.05 at the cobalt lines. These are conditional comparisons using the recovered export's documented percent interpretation, not independently certified efficiencies.

In-114m also has different gamma inputs: QG prints 15.56%, 4.40%, 4.40% at 190/558/725 keV; the bundled analysis uses 18.6%, 3.50%, 3.50%. A source-library qualification is needed before choosing a winner.

Sc-48's roughly 98% summary differences are the existing printed intensity/line-summary problem. Those three cases are excluded from clean incidence and need the saved declared intensity scenarios. Additional inferred Ni-57, Ti-51 and Mn-54 outputs lack QG summary targets and are not newly accepted identifications.

Short-lived time references remain relevant: count-start/count-average factors are about 1.08 for In-115m, and 1.017 or 1.221 for Sc-47's four-hour or 48-hour counts. Under the count-start interpretation these corrections increase the positive differences. No automatic timezone adjustment was made.

Reporting and next work
The line CSV labels comparison_net_counts as raw_net_counts alongside activity calculated from physical net_counts. This can misclassify measured-background loss as efficiency_or_activity_conversion_bias. Both counts and their bases must be explicit, with an algebraically consistent conversion audit.

IEC/Covell/Gilmore controls leave Co-Cd activity at 156.231 Bq under North while the comparison candidate changes. The residual ROI/fit drives activity in this branch; selected comparison methods must not be mistaken for activity-method selection. This behavior requires documentation/review, not blindly substituting ambient-containing comparison counts into activity.

Resolve background applicability/QG background settings first, make count bases explicit, and then compare source-bound efficiency, yield and time-reference scenarios. Retain the historical and no-background controls. The existing FluxForge repair task owns implementation.
